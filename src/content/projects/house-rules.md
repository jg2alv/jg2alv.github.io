House Rules investigates how income, ocean proximity, housing age, and geography relate to median property values in California’s 1990 census data. Working as a team, we organized the notebook around research questions that connect a real-world subject to exploratory analysis and statistical methods.

My contribution was data cleaning and exploratory data analysis. Using pandas and interactive Plotly visualizations, I examined distributions and relationships through scatter plots, violin plots, and geographic views. The exploration also highlighted an important dataset limitation: property values are capped near $500,000, which constrains how the upper end of the market can be interpreted.

A central analytical challenge was distinguishing patterns in the plots from stronger statistical claims. The team’s notebook includes correlation analyses and a group comparison, alongside a separate regression demonstration built with synthetic data—not a validated housing-price predictor. The project strengthened my ability to prepare data, select informative visualizations, and communicate findings without confusing association with causation.
